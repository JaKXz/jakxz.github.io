# Vale is an editor, not a rewriter

The first Vale pass on “I attended the church of jj” produced no spelling or
repetition errors, 11 warnings, and 29 suggestions. Most suggestions came from
`write-good.E-Prime`, which objects to forms of “to be.” That constraint does
not describe Jason’s established voice and should not drive a rewrite.

## Actionable prompts

- `House.MetaOpening` identifies “This post will…” as a container statement
  rather than the arguable position lesson 1 asks for.
- “Aforementioned” is worth replacing if the sentence can point to the relevant
  tutorials more directly.
- “Several,” “huge,” and “very” are worth checking wherever a concrete detail
  could make the stakes clearer.

## Intentional voice

- “Under the weather” establishes the Sunday scene economically. Its familiarity
  is not automatically a defect.
- “Very.” works as comic timing after “How painful would this be in git?” A rule
  about weasel words does not understand that exchange.
- First person, conversational asides, fragments, and forms of “to be” need no
  blanket correction.

## Vocabulary

The project vocabulary removes false spelling alerts for the technical language
already used across the blog, including `jj`, `repo`, `pseudocode`, `VSCode`,
and `LLMs`. New technical terms should be added only after checking that an
alert is not exposing a typo or incorrect product casing.

## Implication

Use Vale after Jason writes the new opening from memory. Compare its alerts with
the lesson’s structural goals, then revise only where both forms of evidence
point to a clearer sentence.
