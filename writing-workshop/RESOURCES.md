# Argumentative technical writing resources

## Knowledge

- [Sean Goedecke: “Writing a tech blog people want to read”](https://www.seangoedecke.com/on-writing/)
  Sean’s direct account of his goals, tone, use of experience, attitude toward
  caveats, and drafting process. Use for: distinguishing transferable writing
  choices from surface imitation.
- [Sean Goedecke: “Blog about things you don’t understand yet”](https://www.seangoedecke.com/blog-about-things-you-dont-understand-yet/)
  His clearest explanation of treating every post as an argument and using
  writing to discover what he thinks. Use for: thesis formation and revision.
- [Sean Goedecke: “Good writing is obvious, not original”](https://www.seangoedecke.com/good-writing-is-obvious-not-original/)
  A primary-source argument for articulating the beliefs already embedded in
  real experience. Use for: finding ideas without manufacturing novelty.
- [Sean Goedecke: “How I ship projects at big tech companies”](https://www.seangoedecke.com/how-to-ship/)
  A representative experience-driven essay with an early position, concrete
  mechanisms, objections, and a compressed summary. Use for: structural study.
- [Sean Goedecke: “Mistakes engineers make in large established codebases”](https://www.seangoedecke.com/large-established-codebases/)
  A representative essay that names one cardinal claim, supports it with
  mechanisms and examples, then handles competing concerns. Use for: proof
  chains and section order.
- [Vale](https://vale.sh/)
  A markup-aware prose linter. The repository’s configuration skips Markdown
  syntax and code while applying a local vocabulary and house style. Use for:
  a repeatable final review after the argument and voice are already working.
- [write-good for Vale](https://vale.sh/explorer/write-good/)
  An opinionated collection of checks for passive voice, weasel words,
  clichés, and related patterns. Use for: questions worth investigating, not
  rules that every sentence must obey.
- [Jason Kurian: “TDD doesn’t have to be dogmatic”](../src/lib/posts/tdd-does-not-have-to-be-dogmatic.md)
  A strong example of Jason stating a provocative position immediately while
  retaining humor and practical detail. Use for: Jason’s existing voice.
- [Jason Kurian: “Pairing”](../src/lib/posts/pairing.md)
  A longer example of Jason organizing practical experience into explicit
  principles. Use for: instruction, confidence, and section structure.
- [Jason Kurian: “Reviewing code: familiar or not”](../src/lib/posts/reviewing-code-familiar-or-not.md)
  A grounded argument whose authority comes from disclosed experience and
  specific advice. Use for: audience, credibility, and concrete examples.
- [Raw jj working notes](../src/todo.md)
  The source material behind the draft’s aliases, stack operations, and
  conceptual discoveries. Use for: facts and memories, not ready-made prose.
- [Jujutsu: Working copy](https://docs.jj-vcs.dev/latest/working-copy/)
  Primary documentation for `@`, automatic snapshots, and the working-copy
  commit. Use for: checking the essay’s central explanation.
- [Jujutsu: CLI reference](https://docs.jj-vcs.dev/latest/cli-reference/)
  Primary documentation for `squash --into`, `git push`, and `undo`. Use for:
  command accuracy.
- [Jujutsu: First-class conflicts](https://docs.jj-vcs.dev/latest/conflicts/)
  Primary documentation for automatic descendant rebasing and recorded
  conflicts. Use for: explaining why stack rewriting feels different.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [Lobsters](https://lobste.rs/)
  A technically experienced audience useful for testing whether an argument is
  precise and whether caveats are genuinely necessary.
- [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/)
  A broad technical audience useful for discovering which interpretations and
  objections a published essay invites. Participation is optional; nothing is
  posted as part of this workshop.
