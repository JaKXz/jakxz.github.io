Lesson 01 · Thesis · About 15 minutes

Find the sentence you will defend

Your draft already has a scene, stakes, and a memorable example. The missing piece is the sentence that tells those details what job to do.

You already know how to do this

Your post “TDD doesn’t have to be dogmatic” opens with a recognizable piece of your voice:

Write your tests first!
Or don’t!
It doesn’t matter.

It immediately follows that provocation with a testable position: what makes development test-driven is the resulting understanding and confidence, not ritual obedience to one sequence. The rest of the post can then demonstrate that position.

Your jj draft currently opens its argument differently:

This post will not be a full blown tutorial on jj.

That sentence defines the container. It does not yet tell the reader what you believe. A format is not a thesis.

The transferable Sean Goedecke technique

Sean says that each post he publishes argues a point, and that a draft with nothing a sensible reader could dispute is usually not worth publishing. He also describes his popular posts as clear opinions grounded in actual work. Those are structural choices; they do not require adopting his voice.

Primary sources: “Blog about things you don’t understand yet” and “Writing a tech blog people want to read.”

  1. Name the pressure.
    What real situation forced you to care?
  2. Make a judgment.
    What do you now believe that another capable engineer might resist?
  3. Expose the mechanism.
    Why did the tool change the outcome?

The target meaning—not final prose

Your chosen argument is that jj fits the reality of stacked changes better than Git’s usual branch-and-rewrite workflow. “It took fewer commands” is evidence. It is not the whole claim.

Write the new opening

Write no more than 140 words in three short paragraphs. Do this from the memory of your experience, not by editing the existing introduction line by line.

  1. Scene: Sunday morning, being ill, and the dread of Monday.
  2. Pressure: why the stacked PRs mattered and why changing their base felt dangerous.
  3. Position: the one sentence about jj you are willing to defend.

One alert already matters

The house rule flags “This post will…” because it announces a format instead of advancing a claim. That corroborates the lesson. By contrast, write-good objects to “under the weather” and “Very.” Those may be intentional parts of your scene and comic timing.

For each alert, write down one of three decisions: actionable, intentional voice, or vocabulary. Accept a change only if you can name what it improves.

Stop here

Copy your opening and send it to Codex for feedback. Do not revise the rest of the post yet. Lesson 2 will be designed from what this attempt shows about your current understanding.

Ask follow-up questions whenever a distinction or piece of feedback is unclear. The feedback loop is part of the lesson.
